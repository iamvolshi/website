The filing covers eleven mechanisms:

  1. The sealed record — every belief append-only, hash-chained to the entry before it. Tampering, deletion, or a broken interval is detected by verification, which identifies the exact affected row.
  2. Knowledge time — a third clock: not when an event happened, and not when it arrived, but when it was known, and at what authority.
  3. Authority that falls without erasing — a wrong belief remains in the record while its authority collapses. The system learns without rewriting its history.
  4. Born registered — every kernel is assigned a Kernel ID at birth. No KID, no read, no write. What is not yet understood runs as null: registered, present, and unassigned — never invisible.
  5. The poison proof — the record verifies its own integrity against tampering, deletion, and interval attacks, and produces proof of each catch.
  6. The firewall — each source carries a damage accumulator. Past a threshold, the source continues to be recorded in full and ceases to have any effect. The attack is preserved as evidence.
  7. The self-healing loop — the machine reads its own record, identifies a deficiency, constructs the missing component, and integrates it.
  8. Parameters as deeds — a change to a kernel's parameters is recorded as a versioned event on the same identity. No component receives a new identity to escape its history.
  9. Snapshots, not state — any view of the present is derived from the ledger and can be re-derived identically at any time. Where a snapshot and the ledger disagree, the ledger governs.
  10. Pressure-pot attention — influence the system cannot consolidate accumulates per kind until it crosses an attention threshold. Attention discharges it.
  11. Debt and the curve — outstanding deficit removes options from the injured component until it heals; a component's position on the response curve is set by design, as protection, as a sensor, or for deliberate retention.
