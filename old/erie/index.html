How she works

A question goes in. An epistemic result comes out.

ERIE sits between the question and the answer. She derives what would count as an answer, plans the evidence she'd need, retrieves it, and judges whether it's sufficient — sufficient, insufficient, contradictory, stale, or ambiguous. Then she reports.

01

Target

The question becomes a target with satisfaction criteria — what an answer would have to establish before anyone gets to speak.

02

Evidence

Retrieval runs through an adapter over your data pool. Every candidate is bound back to the criteria it supports, with its provenance attached.

03

Verdict

Sufficiency is judged, not assumed. Answered — with the full chain of custody. Or cannot-answer — with the gaps preserved, not paved over.

The honesty boundary

She won't invent. She won't vouch for the pool, either.

ERIE guarantees the chain of custody — source to evidence to answer — and she would rather say cannot-answer than fabricate. What she does not do is independently verify that your data pool tells the truth; that's the pool's job. In our stack the pool is KATIE's K-record — append-only, every entry bound to its moment — so the ground she stands on is solid. We say exactly this much, and no more.
