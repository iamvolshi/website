The Architecture

Memory isn't a feature. It's the substrate.

Most systems treat memory as storage — a place to put things. ARCHE treats it the way humans do: memory is part of cognition itself. What the system remembers, how it weighs it, and when it reconsiders — that's not a database layer, that's the mind. The tritemporal work underneath (event time, tick time, knowledge time) runs on TEMPE, the time engine — a cognitive model of human memory, not a storage optimization.

ERIE — Epistemic Retrieval & Investigation Engine

Retrieval without hallucination. When a bot asks about your user, everything that comes back carries its provenance. No invented answers. No confident fiction. And when the evidence isn't there, she says so — explicitly.

HOW SHE WORKS — 3-STAGE EPISTEMIC INQUEST
01 / TARGET

The question becomes a target with satisfaction criteria — what an answer would have to establish before anyone gets to speak.

02 / EVIDENCE

Retrieval runs through an adapter over your data pool. Every candidate is bound back to the criteria it supports, with its provenance attached.

03 / VERDICT

Sufficiency is judged, not assumed. Answered — with the full chain of custody. Or cannot-answer — with the gaps preserved, not paved over.

erie.ask — example
Real engine output shape; the data below is illustrative.
> erie.ask("Why was acct-002's name changed?") STATUS: ANSWERED
evidence: k-record interval [k2..live] · cust_name = “Sofia Bennett”
provenance: katie.entries row 47 · reason “married” · authority 89%
gaps: none — criteria satisfied
> erie.ask("What is the customer's mother's maiden name?") STATUS: CANNOT-ANSWER
gaps: no evidence in the pool addresses this · refused to invent.
shield The Honesty Boundary

ERIE guarantees the chain of custody — source to evidence to answer — and she would rather say cannot-answer than fabricate. What she does not do is independently verify that your data pool tells the truth; that's the pool's job. In our stack the pool is KATIE's K-record — append-only, every entry bound to its moment — so the ground she stands on is solid. We say exactly this much, and no more.

ELLE — External Learning Loop Engine

She learns. You never start from zero again. Every interaction is an experience, and experience accumulates: lessons learned, scoped, attributed — durable rules that survive model replacement and follow the user into whatever model they talk to next.

HER JOB — 4-STEP LEARNING LOOP
01 / EXPERIENCE

Every interaction with the user — lived, not logged. What happened, in context.

02 / LESSON

Experience distills into a lesson: what was learned, scoped to where it holds, attributed to its source.

03 / RULE

Lessons mature into rules — durable, portable, surviving whatever model comes next.

04 / RETURN

The rules follow the user into every future interaction. Never start from zero again.

Why External?

The learning doesn't live inside the model. Models get replaced. Fine-tuning gets wiped. Context windows get flushed. ELLE's loop is external — the lessons live outside any single model, so the user's accumulated experience survives all of it. Swap the model underneath; the learning stays.

Scoped

Every lesson knows where it applies — a lesson about one user doesn't leak into another.

Attributed

Every lesson carries where it came from — the experience it was learned in, on the record.

Durable

Lessons outlive models. Replacement, retraining, new vendor — the loop doesn't care.

TEMPE · The time engine

The day is the only constant.

TEMPE is the time engine — the clockwork the record runs on. Every timestamp in the system comes from it: when things happened, and when the system knew them.

ONE CONSTANT

The day is the only constant. Weeks, months, quarters, and years are containers made of days. They can split and spill. The day count never resets.

THE STOPWATCH

Record timestamps come from a stopwatch clock: forward-only, resuming from the highest tick on boot, resetting daily at local midnight. Real time is the wall clock. Event time is built on it. Knowledge time is the stopwatch.

THE FRACTAL YEAR

A fractal year is 365 days. A Gregorian leap year is a 366-day container, and the remainder day is day 365 — it sits in the week straddling the year boundary, not outside the week.

TEMPE and Kronos are the same thing: the time engine, by either name.
